# What does it actually take to make a brand AI-ready?

Design research from [iO](https://www.iodigital.com). We invented a drinks brand, asked AI to
make real things with it — packaging, campaign copy, product shots — and measured where it broke.

**Read it:** https://michasymoens-ux.github.io/uur-ai-ready/

Two pages:

- **`index.html`** — the story. Six beats, what broke and why it matters.
- **`index_full_record.html`** — every test, every number, every caveat. For anyone who wants to
  challenge a claim rather than read one.

## What this is

"Is your brand AI-ready?" is sold everywhere and defined nowhere. So rather than write another
opinion, we built **UUR** — an invented non-alcoholic drinks brand named after the hour — designed
it properly, rebuilt it as something a machine can read, and ran tests that could actually fail.

The findings in one line each:

- Never let AI draw your logo. Build the artwork, then wrap it onto the thing.
- A gap in your brand kit doesn't produce obvious mistakes. It produces confident fakes.
- Your examples carry everything you never thought to write down.
- Stop asking "is my brand AI-ready?" Ask "who makes this, and who checks it?"
- An automatic check only checks what you remembered to ask.

The last one is the uncomfortable ending: our own automated check passed a label whose wordmark
read "KLOK" in a serif. We publish that because a method that hides its own blind spot isn't one.

## Status

Work in progress, published deliberately. Gaps are printed rather than rounded up — motion has no
evidence and no owner yet, type licensing is open, and the packaging file shown uses a stand-in
typeface and placeholder colours. It demonstrates the method, not a finished brand.
